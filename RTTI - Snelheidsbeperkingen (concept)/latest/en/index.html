Introduction to semantic modelling

A concept scheme (in SKOS: ConceptScheme) is a structured collection of concepts that carry a fixed, shared meaning within a particular domain. The goal is for everyone working with the model — people and systems alike — to use the same words for the same things. That sounds obvious, but in practice language causes a surprising amount of confusion: what one organisation calls a measurement point, another calls a sensor, a location, or an observation station.

A concept scheme solves this by recording, for each concept:

  • a preferred label (prefLabel) it is known by,
  • a definition that unambiguously describes what the concept does and does not cover,
  • and optionally a scope note or example to clarify borderline cases.

Concepts rarely stand on their own. They are related hierarchically (a vehicle is broader than a truck) or associatively (a road user relates to a road segment). These relationships make it possible for queries and analyses to navigate between general and specific concepts, and to make connections between concepts explicit. Within SKOS we use broader, narrower, and related for that.

When a concept also exists elsewhere — in a national or European standard, for instance — we record that as a mapping relation. This prevents reinventing the wheel: we link to the source so interoperability with external systems remains possible.

This document describes the concept scheme as it is currently recorded. The following sections list the metadata, every concept, the relationships between them, and — where the model is connected to a data source — how it is linked to that source data.

Inner regime

Concept details
FieldValue
ScopeThe inner zone concept describes the cascading effect of nested zones. When entering an inner zone, the speed restriction of the enclosing zone is temporarily suspended. When exiting, the speed restriction of the enclosing zone becomes active again. This mechanism works recursively: multiple levels of nesting are possible.

Time window

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionThe days and/or hours indicated on a supplementary sign that imply a limitation of the temporal scope of application of the indication on the overlying traffic sign; in particular, for speed limits, the period during which the maximum speed indicated on the traffic sign applies.
ScopeThe term 'time window' does not appear as such in the legislation; the legal basis lies in the limitation of the scope and is referred to as a 'time window' by Rijkswaterstaat and CROW. The concept has a dual realization: as a physical supplementary sign beneath a metal sign, or as a time condition within a digital/electronic sign. Not to be confused with 'window times' (loading and unloading times for urban logistics), which are incorporated into the Goods Delivery model.

Line geometry

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionA one-dimensional geometric primitive representing a line: a continuous curve, measurable in length within a coordinate system, composed of one or more connected line segments in which the end point of each segment — except the last — coincides with the start point of the next. Line geometry is used to spatially represent linear features such as a road; designated GM_Curve in ISO 19107 and Curve/LineString in OGC Simple Features

Concept details
FieldValue
DefinitionWritten decision by the competent authority deciding on the placement, modification, or removal of traffic signs and supplementary signs designated by Order in Council insofar as this creates or modifies a command or prohibition, or on measures on or along the road that lead to a restriction or expansion of the number of categories of road users.
ScopePublication takes place pursuant to Article 26 of the BABW via the KOOP Traffic Decisions application in the Government Gazette (National Government) or the local publication journal (municipality, province, water board) since the entry into force of the Electronic Publications Act on 1 July 2021. Requirements for justification follow from Article 21 of the BABW; consultation with the Chief of Police follows from Article 24 of the BABW.
