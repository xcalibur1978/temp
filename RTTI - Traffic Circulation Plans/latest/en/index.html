RTTI — Traffic Circulation Plans

SKOS concept scheme for the priority data type 'traffic circulation plan' under Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/670 (RTTI), supplementing ITS Directive 2010/40/EU as amended by Directive (EU) 2023/2661. The plan-level concept is the single top concept; constituent regulation and zone concepts are arranged beneath it. Definitions follow the authoritative source wording (legally enumerated data types are normative per the Annex of (EU) 2022/670; prose definitions from the NAPCORE ITS Data Dictionary are indicative). Concepts that have a canonical home in a sibling RTTI scheme are referenced via rdfs:seeAlso rather than duplicated.

Introduction to semantic modelling

A concept scheme (in SKOS: ConceptScheme) is a structured collection of concepts that carry a fixed, shared meaning within a particular domain. The goal is for everyone working with the model — people and systems alike — to use the same words for the same things. That sounds obvious, but in practice language causes a surprising amount of confusion: what one organisation calls a measurement point, another calls a sensor, a location, or an observation station.

A concept scheme solves this by recording, for each concept:

  • a preferred label (prefLabel) it is known by,
  • a definition that unambiguously describes what the concept does and does not cover,
  • and optionally a scope note or example to clarify borderline cases.

Concepts rarely stand on their own. They are related hierarchically (a vehicle is broader than a truck) or associatively (a road user relates to a road segment). These relationships make it possible for queries and analyses to navigate between general and specific concepts, and to make connections between concepts explicit. Within SKOS we use broader, narrower, and related for that.

When a concept also exists elsewhere — in a national or European standard, for instance — we record that as a mapping relation. This prevents reinventing the wheel: we link to the source so interoperability with external systems remains possible.

This document describes the concept scheme as it is currently recorded. The following sections list the metadata, every concept, the relationships between them, and — where the model is connected to a data source — how it is linked to that source data.
